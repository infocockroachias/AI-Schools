The Verify-It routine — how analysts meet a machine-made claim
Generative AI produces fluent text, images and voices. Fluency is not evidence. This three-phase routine decides what earns your trust — and what earns a slow, careful "no".
PHASE 1 MEET THE CLAIM slow down — confidence is not proof
Say it preciselyWhat exactly is being claimed? Write it as one checkable sentence.
Name the sourceWho or what produced it — a person, a model, a forwarded bundle of both?
Spot the stakesWhat happens if you repeat it and it is false?
↓
PHASE 2 RUN THE THREE CHECKS
1 · Source checkCan the origin be found? Anonymous = heavy penalty.
2 · Evidence checkIs there a date, a place, a number, a document — or only confident adjectives?
3 · Agreement checkDo two independent, reliable sources agree?
↓
PHASE 3 VERDICT
SHARE — with the source attachedAll three checks pass. You pass the claim on with its evidence, never naked.
DOUBT — and say whyAny check fails. You reply "unverified", keep the receipt, and help the next person doubt faster.
ANALYST'S NOTE: a text generator predicts likely words — it never checks truth. The routine above is not extra homework; it is the difference between reading AI output and relying on it.
