Four principles, one AI case — the ethics review frame
T10-15 runs a medical-triage tool through all four. Each principle is a question with teeth — a design can pass two and fail the others.
RESPECT FOR PERSONS
1

Autonomy

Do affected people know and choose — or is the system deciding over their heads?

In the triage case: patients and doctors must be able to see, question and overrule the model's ranking. A hidden score a doctor cannot contest fails autonomy even if it is accurate.

DO GOOD
2

Beneficence

Does it actually make the outcome better — measured, not promised?

In the triage case: compare real results with and without the tool: shorter waits, fewer missed cases? "The hospital uses AI" is not a benefit; evidence is.

DO NO HARM
3

Non-maleficence

Who absorbs the errors — and could they be the people with least power already?

In the triage case: a false negative sends a sick patient home. Check the confusion matrix per group: a tool that misses illness mostly in one community is causing harm, not just error.

FAIR SHARES
4

Justice

Are benefits and burdens distributed fairly across groups?

In the triage case: if the training data came mostly from city hospitals, rural patients may be ranked lower purely by unfamiliarity. Justice asks for equal quality of service — not equal treatment by a biased machine.

THE ANALYST'S HABIT:

Legal is not the same as ethical. A triage tool can obey every rule in the book and still fail all four principles — which is exactly why audits, transparency and accountability exist. Run the four questions before the deployment, not after the harm.
