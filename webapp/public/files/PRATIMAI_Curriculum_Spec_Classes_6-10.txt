# PRATIMAI — Common AI Curriculum, Classes 6–10
**Version 1.0 · Research cut-off 6 October 2026 · Companion to `AI_Handouts_Creation.md`**
**Purpose:** one coherent, board-neutral AI-*understanding* curriculum that sits underneath the handouts for Classes 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 and maps to CBSE, NCERT/NCF-SE, ICSE (CISCE) and state-board expectations.

> **Core stance.** AI here is a *way of understanding how machines learn from data and how humans should judge them* — not "computers" and not "coding". Every class works mainly with paper, people, patterns, data and arguments.

### 1.2 CBSE AI-literacy syllabus, Classes 6–8 (4 topics × 5 hours) [V — 4 sources agree]

| | Class 6 | Class 7 | Class 8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Topic 1 | Intro to AI & everyday examples: AI vs automation; human vs machine intelligence; supervised / unsupervised / reinforcement learning | AI domains: classification, regression, clustering; computer vision, NLP, data science; chatbots, image recognition, translation | AI project lifecycle: define problem → collect data → test AI tools → reflect & improve |
| Topic 2 | Basic data concepts: types (numbers, text, images, sound); tables/charts | AI in industries: healthcare, education, transport, communication | Deeper applications: environment, healthcare, automation, education; **no-code AI tools** |
| Topic 3 | Simple pattern recognition & decision making | Data visualisation & analysis (bar, line, pie) | Data and fairness: bias in datasets; strategies for fairness |
| Topic 4 | Ethics & digital responsibility: safety, privacy, passwords, digital footprints | Ethics & AI bias awareness; digital citizenship | Ethics & responsible AI: privacy, misinformation, social impact |

Curricular goals for 6–8 (CG-1 CT skills · CG-2 spatial/visual reasoning · CG-3 foundational AI knowledge · CG-4 ethics in AI · CG-5 computer proficiency) [V]. **Note:** the CBSE framework names **no** programming language, platform, or generative-AI tool for Classes 3–8.

### 1.3 CBSE AI Code 417 — Class 9 (2025-26 official curriculum) [O]

Part B units (hours): (1) AI Reflection, Project Cycle & Ethics 30 · (2) Data Literacy 22 · (3) Math for AI — Statistics & Probability 12 · (4) Introduction to Generative AI 8 · (5) Introduction to Python 1+9. Activities include the 4Ws problem canvas, system maps, stakeholder mapping, graph choice, ethics role-play, "Moral Machine", Balloon Debate, number-pattern and picture-analogy tasks, car-spotting data collection, "Real vs AI-generated image" game.

### 1.4 CBSE AI Code 417 — Class 10 (2026-27 official curriculum) [O]

Part B units: (1) Revisiting AI Project Cycle & **Ethical Frameworks** (incl. bioethics case study) · (2) **Advanced Modelling** — AI/ML/DL; rule-based vs learning-based; supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement; classification, regression, clustering, association; ANN & CNN; "How does AI make a decision?"; Human Neural Network game · (3) **Evaluating Models** — train-test split, accuracy, error, confusion matrix, precision, recall, F1, ethical concerns (bias, transparency) · (4) Statistical Data (no-code tools, assessed by practicals) · (5) **Computer Vision** — pixels, grayscale/RGB, convolution, CNN · (6) **NLP** — features of language, stages (lexicon, syntax, semantics), chatbots (script vs smart), text normalisation, bag-of-words, TF-IDF, sentiment analysis · (7) Advanced Python (practicals). Employability skills (50 h) are a separate Part A.

### 1.7 Global frameworks used to anchor the curriculum

| Framework | Key idea we use |
|---|---|
| **UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Students (2024)** [O-listing] | 12 competencies × 4 dimensions (**Human-centred mindset · Ethics of AI · AI techniques & applications · AI system design**) × 3 progression levels (**Understand → Apply → Create**). Vision: students as *co-creators and responsible citizens* |
| **AILit Framework (European Commission + OECD, 2025)** [N] | 4 domains of interaction: **Engaging with AI · Creating with AI · Managing AI · Designing AI**; 19 competences (knowledge, skills, attitudes). Basis of PISA 2029 Media & AI Literacy |
| **AI4K12 "Five Big Ideas"** (AAAI/CSTA) | **Perception · Representation & Reasoning · Learning · Natural Interaction · Societal Impact** — the standard K-12 conceptual spine (also referenced in CBSE AI-for-All material) |
| **Computational Thinking** (CBSE: decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, algorithm design, + data analysis, troubleshooting) | The thinking skills beneath AI; kept as a cross-cutting thread |

### 1.8 Gap analysis → what PRATIMAI adds

| Gap in current boards | Our response |
|---|---|
| Classes 9–10 syllabi are tool- and Python-heavy; "why does it work?" is thin | Paper-based *mechanism* tasks (nearest-neighbour by hand, human neural network, convolution by hand, confusion matrix) |
| Classes 6–8 official syllabus names **no generative-AI content**, yet students use chatbots daily | Conceptual GenAI literacy: Class 6 "confident but wrong", Class 8 "next-word guessing", Class 9 full unit — **no tool required** |
| "Ethics" is often a lecture | Role-plays, audits, debates, decision journals — ethics as *practice* |
| Uncertainty/probability is absent until Class 9 | "How sure am I?" confidence habit from Class 6 |
| Heavy anthropomorphism in popular AI media ("AI thinks") | Language rules: models *predict, match, estimate*; **people** design, choose data and are responsible |
| Large classes, few devices | ≥ 80–100 % of tasks doable with paper, pencil, cards and classmates |

### 1.9 Deliberately excluded
Programming (Python/Scratch) lessons · tool tutorials or brand promotion · maths beyond the class's NCERT level · model-architecture detail beyond Class 10 CBSE scope · speculative "AI will take over" content · any task requiring students to upload photos/voice or create accounts.

## 2. The common framework: Five Strands × Five Levels

### 2.1 The five strands (identical in every class)

| Code | Strand | Core question | AI4K12 | UNESCO dimension |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **U** | **Understand AI** | What is AI, and what is it *not*? | Representation & Reasoning | AI techniques & applications |
| **D** | **Data** | What is data, where does it come from, can I trust it? | Perception | AI techniques / Ethics |
| **L** | **Learn (how machines learn)** | How can a machine improve from examples? How do we know it works? | Learning | AI techniques & applications; System design |
| **W** | **World (AI in use)** | Where does AI act, and how does it see, read, speak, recommend, generate? | Natural Interaction | AI techniques & applications |
| **R** | **Responsibility** | Who decides, who is affected, who is accountable? | Societal Impact | Human-centred mindset; Ethics of AI |

Cross-cutting thread **T — Thinking tools**: decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, algorithms, troubleshooting (CBSE CT) + the **Question Habit** ("How do I know? What is missing? Who made this?").

### 2.2 The five-level spiral (one level per class)

| Class | Level name | Learner verb | UNESCO level | One-sentence aim |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **6** | **Notice** | spot, sort, ask | Understand | "I can see where AI is and tell it from ordinary machines." |
| **7** | **Sort & Predict** | classify, estimate, compare | Understand → Apply | "I can explain how a machine learns from examples — and how it gets fooled." |
| **8** | **Build the Cycle** | plan, test, improve | Apply | "I can plan an AI solution on paper and check it for fairness." |
| **9** | **Reason** | calculate, justify, critique | Apply → Create | "I can use data and maths to explain why AI works and where it fails." |
| **10** | **Evaluate & Design** | measure, decide, defend | Create | "I can judge an AI system with evidence and design a responsible one." |

### 2.3 Concept spiral (how each idea deepens)

| Idea | Class 6 | Class 7 | Class 8 | Class 9 | Class 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **What AI is** | Rules vs learning; human vs machine | 3 learning jobs (classify / predict / group) | AI vs ML; project cycle | AI ⊃ ML ⊃ DL; rule-based vs learning-based | Formal model families; neural nets |
| **Data** | 4 kinds; tables, charts; pixels as numbers | Surveys; chart reading; structured data | Representation, sampling, consent | Data literacy, quality, privacy vs security | Statistical data; train/test split |
| **Learning** | Learn from examples; garbage in/out | Training vs testing; too few examples | Nearest-example classifier by hand | k-nearest neighbours; line of best fit | Neural net by hand; convolution; bag-of-words |
| **Uncertainty** | "How sure am I?" | Prediction can be wrong | Accuracy as % correct | Probability; average vs spread | Precision, recall, F1; base-rate trap |
| **Seeing/Reading** | Picture = grid of numbers | Camera/word-counter ideas | Image classifier as tool | Features & similarity | Convolution; text normalisation; TF-IDF |
| **Generative AI** | "Confident but wrong" | Fake/edited media checks | Next-word guessing; hallucination | Unit: how it works, limits, ethics | Responsible use; evaluation of claims |
| **Responsibility** | Privacy habits; verify | Bias from examples; who is responsible | Fairness checklist; responsible-use cases | Stakeholders; ethics reasoning; access & divide | Ethical frameworks; audit; rights; careers |
| **Thinking tools** | Step-by-step instructions | If-then; elimination | Decompose; optimise | Flowchart / pseudocode trace | Design a full pipeline |

### 2.4 Vocabulary budget (max *new technical terms* per handout)
Class 6: **10** · Class 7: **14** · Class 8: **16** · Class 9: **20** · Class 10: **24**. Each term is introduced *after* an experience, defined in ≤ 15 words, and reused ≥ 3 times.

### 2.5 Length & time (hard rules)
| Class | Target pages | **Hard cap** | CBSE-aligned sessions (≈40–45 min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | 36 | 48 | 20 AI sessions + project sessions |
| 7 | 40 | 48 | 20 + project |
| 8 | 42 | 48 | 20 + project |
| 9 | 44 | 48 | 24–30 |
| 10 | 46 | 48 | 30–40 |
*(The brief says "< 50 pages"; the hard cap of 48 leaves a safety margin for printing/inserts.)*

## 3. Class-by-class curriculum

Each class section has: learner snapshot → big question → chapters (with page budget and board mapping) → learning outcomes (coded) → task bank → misconceptions to fix → assessment.
Outcome code format: `Class.Strand.Number` (e.g., `6.U1`). Task code: `T6-01`. All tasks are **unplugged** (paper, pencil, cards, classmates) unless marked 🖥 *(optional teacher-led extension)*.

### CLASS 6 — "AI Around Me"  ·  Level: NOTICE  ·  ages 11–12

**Big question:** *Is a machine ever really smart?*  **Handout length:** target 36 pp (cap 48).

**Chapters, pages and mapping**

| # | Chapter (working title) | Pages | CBSE Class 6 topic | UNESCO / AILit | Strand |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Front matter: cover, "How to use this handout", journey map | 3 | — | — | — |
| 1 | **Machines That Seem Smart** — rule-following vs learning; humans vs machines; the three ways of learning (as stories) | 7 | T1 Intro to AI & everyday examples | Engage with AI; human-centred mindset | U, W |
| 2 | **Data: The Food of AI** — numbers, words, pictures, sound; tables, charts; a picture as a grid of numbers; garbage in, garbage out | 7 | T2 Basic data concepts | Understand data | D |
| 3 | **Patterns & Decisions** — finding and using patterns; if-then; yes/no question trees; "how sure am I?" | 7 | T3 Simple pattern recognition & decision making | AI techniques | L, T |
| 4 | **Be a Smart Digital Citizen** — privacy, passwords, footprints, kindness, real vs fake | 6 | T4 Ethics & digital responsibility | Ethics of AI; Manage AI | R |
| 5 | **Capstone: My Neighbourhood AI Investigation** + reflection | 4 | Interdisciplinary project hours | Create | all |
| 6 | Back matter: glossary (≤10 terms), my progress, certificate/page | 2 | — | — | — |
| | **Total** | **36** | | | |

**Learning outcomes**
- **6.U1** Explain in own words that AI learns patterns from examples, and tell it apart from a fixed-rule machine using 3 daily examples.
- **6.U2** Name two things humans do better than machines and two things machines do better, with reasons.
- **6.D1** Name four kinds of data (numbers, words, pictures, sounds); sort data into a table; draw a bar chart.
- **6.D2** Show how a simple picture can be written as numbers on a grid.
- **6.L1** Find a pattern, predict what comes next, and say how sure they are.
- **6.L2** Describe three ways of learning — from labelled examples, by grouping without labels, by trial and reward — using human analogies.
- **6.W1** Point to AI at home, school and in the neighbourhood and say what data it uses.
- **6.R1** Use five safe habits: private info stays private, strong passphrase, think before posting, be kind, check before believing.
- **6.R2** Give one reason an AI answer can be wrong and one way to check it.
- **6.T1** Break a task into exact steps another person can follow.

**Task bank (20 tasks; Ch1–2 first tasks must be "success-first")**

| Code | Task | Mode · min | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| T6-01 | **Human Robot** — one gives exact steps, one follows *literally* | pair · 10 | Rule-following machines; precision (6.T1, 6.U1) |
| T6-02 | **Rule or Learner?** sort 8 machines (washing machine, lift, video app, spam filter…) | pair · 10 | AI vs automation (6.U1) |
| T6-03 | **Teach the Alien** — teach a classmate "mango" using only examples and non-examples; then use only green mangoes | group · 15 | Learning from examples; bias seed (6.U1, 6.L2) |
| T6-04 | **Better At / Worse At** — humans vs machines table, then class vote | alone→class · 10 | Human vs machine intelligence (6.U2) |
| T6-05 | **AI Detective at Home** — tick where AI may hide in a morning routine; find which ones *learn* | home · 15 | Everyday AI (6.W1) |
| T6-06 | **Four Kinds of Data Hunt** in the classroom | pair · 10 | Data types (6.D1) |
| T6-07 | **Table to Chart** — class favourite sport tally → bar chart | group · 15 | Organising data (6.D1) |
| T6-08 | **Pixel Picture** — colour a 8×8 grid from number codes; swap codes, partner redraws | pair · 15 | A picture is numbers (6.D2) |
| T6-09 | **Garbage In, Garbage Out** — mislabel some cards on purpose; watch the learned rule go wrong | group · 10 | Data quality (6.R2) |
| T6-10 | **Pattern Hunt** — number/shape/letter patterns, predict next, rate confidence | alone · 10 | Patterns (6.L1) |
| T6-11 | **Guess My Rule** — secret rule, test with examples, discover it | group · 15 | Learning from examples (6.L1, 6.L2) |
| T6-12 | **20-Questions Tree** — draw a yes/no tree to identify 6 animals; fewer questions wins | pair · 15 | Decisions; if-then (6.T1) |
| T6-13 | **Sure or Not Sure?** — confidence cards for AI-style answers | class · 10 | Uncertainty (6.R2) |
| T6-14 | **Privacy Traffic Light** — share / ask first / never share (using shapes ● ▲ ■ plus colour) | alone · 10 | Privacy (6.R1) |
| T6-15 | **Password Recipe** — build a passphrase on paper (never a real one) | pair · 10 | Safety (6.R1) |
| T6-16 | **Real or Fake Detective** — 3 checks on printed examples | group · 15 | Verification (6.R2) |
| T6-17 | **Footprint Walk** — trace where one imaginary post travels | alone · 10 | Digital footprint (6.R1) |
| T6-18 | **AI in My Neighbourhood** — survey 5 adults: where do you meet "smart" machines? | group project · 2–3 periods | Data collection, summary (6.D1, 6.W1) |
| T6-19 | **My Helper Machine Poster** — design a rule-based or learning helper; list its data and limits | group · 2 periods | Synthesis (6.U1–6.R2) |
| T6-20 | **I used to think… Now I think…** | alone · 10 | Reflection |

**Misconceptions to address:** "AI is a robot" · "AI knows everything / is always right" · "AI is alive / has feelings" · "AI = computers = coding" · "Bigger screen = smarter".
**Subject links:** Maths (patterns, tables, bar graphs), Science (observation & classification), Social Science (community survey), English (instructions).
**Assessment:** formative only — self-check ticks, one-line explanations, Task 18/19 rubric (4 levels: Beginning · Growing · Secure · Shining), teacher observation.

## 5. Assessment philosophy (all classes)
1. **Progress, not marks.** Four mastery levels: **Beginning · Growing · Secure · Shining** (Classes 6–8); **Emerging · Developing · Proficient · Advanced** (9–10).
2. **Three evidence types per chapter:** a *self-check* (confidence), a *performance task* (shows reasoning), a *reflection* ("what changed in my thinking?").
3. **Rubrics reward reasoning** — a correct answer with no explanation scores *Growing*; a flawed answer with sound, honest reasoning can score *Secure*.
4. **No public ranking** of students on AI tasks. Group work gets individual reflection.
5. Teacher pack includes answer key + common-error notes; student handout never shows full solutions on the same page as tasks.
